## 2. Where the screenshots came from — read this before presenting

Every screenshot is the **real application**, captured by driving the live UI in a
browser. Nothing is a mock-up **except** slides 34, 35 and 36, which are explicitly
labelled `PROPOSED · MOCK-UP` on the image itself and in the caption.

The app was run against a **sample dataset**, not production:

- **Real, from the repository:** the 322-location roster, the policy revision dates,
  every checklist item, form reference and instruction text, the JC 427 item list and
  its three-year / six-month expiry rule.
- **Invented for the deck:** all 68 visits, all 462 findings, the follow-up checklists,
  the employee names on the JC 427 tab, the Team Planner entries, and the OP 541
  workbook that gets uploaded on slides 10–11.

So the *shapes* on the trend slides are real behaviour and the *numbers* are not ours.
Slide 25 says this outright, and the trend captions are marked "(sample data)". If
anyone asks whether Cromwell CT really has 60 findings — it does not; that site was
picked by a random number generator.

The three exports described on slides 20–22 were generated by the app during
preparation and opened to confirm the contents, including that the OP 541 write-back
puts the on-site answers into the original workbook's `ON-SITE VISIT` column and
leaves every other part of the file untouched.

## 3. Numbers that are assumptions, not measurements

Slide 39 is the only place the deck puts a figure on the value, and every number in
its middle two columns is an estimate. **Get them replaced before this goes to a
budget conversation.**

| Task | Today (min) | With the app (min) | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visit report and follow-up email | 90 | 5 | Report and email are generated; 5 min is review and send |
| OP 541 / 541T transcription and comparison | 60 | 10 | 10 min is upload plus answering; the comparison is automatic |
| JC 427 review — transcription and expiry dates | 75 | 15 | Scales with employee count; 75 assumes ~8 employees |
| Follow-up list creation and distribution | 45 | 2 | 2 min is generating and sending the link |
| Chasing follow-up status to closure | 60 | 10 | 10 min is reading the dashboard instead of emailing |

Derived from those: **4.8 hrs saved per visit**, and **≈0.5 FTE / 1,150 hrs a year**
at an assumed 240 visits annually. The 240 is itself an assumption — swap in the real
visit count.

Two further claims on that slide are qualitative and should be presented as such:
"Zero licence cost" is true (the app has no per-seat or per-site fee, and runs on
GitHub Pages plus a Firebase project), and "same day report turnaround" is a
capability, not a measured average.

Slide 36's mock-up carries two illustrative figures that the app does **not** capture
today — the corrective-action rate and the open-findings-by-age bands. Closing a
follow-up item is not timestamped yet; adding that timestamp is line 5 of the build
table on slide 37. The mock-up's caption and the view's own footnote both say so.

## 4. The weighting model — starting point for the tiering exercise

The deck asks for approval of the model, not of a finished tier list. This is the
draft the mock-ups were computed from; it is a starting point for the department to
argue with, not a proposal to adopt as written.

**Weights:** Critical ×5, Major ×3, Minor ×1.

**Score:** `100 − (weighted findings ÷ weighted items assessed) × 100`, so the
denominator is weighted the same way as the numerator. A flat percentage-of-items
-passed is dominated by the 100+ routine items every location clears and barely moves.

**Multipliers:** recurrence ×1.5 on a second visit and ×2 on a third or later;
age ×1.25 past 30 days open and ×1.5 past 60 days.

### Draft tier assignment

**Critical** — patient safety, licensure, and anything a surveyor treats as immediate:

- Concentrator/ventilator alarm verification with the patient
- Oxygen flow checked at the end of the longest tubing
- Outlet grounding test; cylinder storage safety
- Ventilator circuit test, battery test, alarm test
- Analyzed concentrator oxygen percentage at minimum run time
- Back-up tank verification and AMA on file
- Expired competency or personnel dates (JC 427 red status)
- TB test / risk assessment; N95 fit testing
- Licences on file; Certificate of Insurance
- EMR reviews (20 semi-annually); facility safety inspection (OP 512)
- FDA 001 smoke alarm checks
- Every OP 541T transfill item — signage, batch records, analyzer calibration
- PAP pressure checked with manometer; magnetic PAP mask risk discussion

**Major** — accreditation documentation that a surveyor will ask to see:

- Binder records: OP 843, OP 519, OP 520, OP 542, OP 752, OP 512
- SDS on file; RM 1234 / RM 1238 training and assessment records
- Competencies expiring within six months (JC 427 amber status)
- Storage and distribution training (FDA 019)
- Hepatitis B test or OP 515 statement
- Location metrics pulled from Tableau and reviewed monthly
- Mandatory annual in-services (SE 800)
- Most OP 541 location-readiness rows

**Minor** — administrative and housekeeping:

- Quarterly newsletters (Safety Matters, Don't Bug Me)
- OP 201 organisational chart; phone listing
- Driver's licence copy; unsigned performance review
- Patient Information Booklet flagging; complaint form stock
- Attendance sheets; filing order; signage placement

Rules of thumb worth agreeing on with the tiers:

1. If a surveyor finding on this item would produce a condition-level citation, it is
   Critical.
2. If the item protects a patient in the home rather than a record in a binder, it is
   at least Major.
3. Tiering is a clinical and regulatory judgement, not a technical one. It should be
   signed off by the department and revisited annually with the standards.

## 5. The one risk to raise yourself

Slide 42 says it: the app is currently maintained by one person. Raising this
unprompted is what makes the rest of the deck credible, and it is the strongest
argument for the third ask — naming a departmental owner and a documented support
path. Do not let it be discovered in Q&A.

## 6. Rebuilding the deck

The deck is generated, not hand-built, so a content change is a code change:

- `scratchpad/build-deck.mjs` — the pptxgenjs generator (layout, copy, slide order)
- `scratchpad/app/mocks/` — the Firebase stand-in, the demo dataset generator, and
  the weighting mock-up generator
- `scratchpad/shoot*.mjs` — the Playwright capture passes

Those live in the working scratchpad rather than the repository, since they exist to
produce one artifact and depend on a patched copy of the app. If the deck needs
regenerating later, the sequence is: seed the demo data, run the app locally with the
Firebase stand-in aliased in, capture the screens, crop the long ones, then run the
generator. Regenerating is only necessary if the app's UI changes — editing the
`.pptx` directly in PowerPoint is fine for wording.
